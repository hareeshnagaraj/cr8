cr8 vision note

cr8 is a new room for unreleased music

A vision note for musical friends: why cr8 is more than a fast private streaming app, and why a million small personal archives could change how musicians and their circles hear, tag, send, and finish work.

Private first The archive can stay close to the artist while the listening layer travels.
Friend-scale The valuable user is not only a producer. It is anyone with taste inside the music’s orbit.
Archive as room The product turns old bounces into a place people can hear, tag, remember, and send.
Many crates The network can grow from small trusted rooms instead of one public feed.
Every artist gets their own crate. Every trusted friend gets a real way to hear and respond. The archive stays close to the person who made it.

The point

cr8 is not trying to be another place to upload songs. The point is stranger and better: it gives unfinished music a room where it can be heard with context, judged in the moment, and sent without dying in a folder.

Most music software treats a song as either a file, a release, a sample, or a post. That misses the middle — which is where most original music actually lives. Demos. Versions. The one bounce that was good. The friend who needed to hear it Tuesday, not “whenever I find the drive.”

That is the larger vision. cr8 is a private room for music that is still becoming itself. Musicians belong in it. So do the people around them who can listen carefully.

Why this had to exist

Every musician has some version of the same broken archive. Demos in folders. Bounces with dates in the filename. Five takes of a song and only one of them is alive. The work is stored. The good ones stay missing when they matter.

Released music has too much infrastructure. Unreleased music has almost none. Streaming is for finished tracks. File share is for shipping bytes. Sample marketplaces are for commerce. None of them are a living room for original work and the circle that cares about it.

The deeper failure is product shape. Tools built for finished catalogs treat metadata as decoration. Tools built for sharing treat listening as a one-shot stream. What original music needs is a place where listening is the work: dig, tag, heart, send, come back.

What changes when friends can really listen

Imagine opening a link from a friend and seeing a real living archive, not a promo page. You can play, dig, heart, tag, leave a note, and come back later. The song doesn’t vanish into a DM thread. The judgment doesn’t require a second app.

That is a different audience from a sample library. Producers still matter — keys, BPM, stems, versions. But the circle around the music matters just as much: bandmates, co-writers, trusted ears. Taste is a first-class user.

The new paradigm: many private crates, lightly connected

The long game is not one giant public music social network. It is many small crates: each artist (or each circle) runs their own room. Friends get invited in. Links cross the fence when someone chooses. The archive never has to become a platform’s inventory.

That is why self-hosting is not a footnote. If the only way to use the software is to upload the corpus into someone else’s business, the product has already lost the point.

What the product is, in plain terms

  • A catalog of your bounces that you can actually search and dig
  • Playback that survives navigation
  • Tags and hearts attributed to real people, while the music plays
  • Shares and invites for a closed circle
  • Optional local tools (stems, enrichment) that never require the cloud

The original files stay read-only. Opinions live in a database. Streams come from a rebuildable mirror. That split is how you move fast without risking years of unreleased work.

How it should feel

Fast enough that a phone far from the machine still feels local. Quiet enough that the UI gets out of the way. Honest empty states. Color from the music (keys, eras), not decoration for its own sake.

If it feels like admin software, it failed. If it feels like a room, it worked.

Why this is not producer-only

Producer tools optimize for making. cr8 optimizes for continuing: hearing what already exists, deciding what it is, and putting it in front of the right person. That is a creative act. It is also a social one.

The sentence

cr8 is open-source software for running your own private room around original music — so unfinished work can be heard, judged, and shared without surrendering the archive.

Who’s behind it

Built by two friends who’ve been making music together for over a decade. We started cr8 because we needed it for ourselves; we open-sourced it because the room should belong to anyone who wants one. Our project is all vars (@allvarsmusic).
